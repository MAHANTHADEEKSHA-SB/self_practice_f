12 · Why each rule exists

Interop§5.1.1 p.184

Support every rate from 4 GT/s up to your maximum

A device supporting 16 GT/s must also run 4, 8 and 12 GT/s.

Why

Two dies from different vendors always share 4 GT/s, and training can step down through every rate when a channel cannot hold the top one (speed degrade in LINKSPEED).

Real-time example

A 32 GT/s die meets a 16 GT/s die: they train at 16. Later the channel degrades and the link falls back to 12 GT/s.

If you break it

A die that skips 12 GT/s strands its partner at 8 GT/s when 16 fails.

Check it in the spec
  • SpecData rates §5.1.1, p.184
Clocking§5.1.1 p.184

One REFCLK, under 5 ns of transport delay difference

Transmitter and partner receiver share a reference clock from one source, with Tx-to-Rx transport delay difference under 5 ns.

Why

With the same reference, both ends run at exactly the same frequency, so the receive FIFO only absorbs phase, not frequency drift. Limiting the delay difference keeps that FIFO shallow and its latency low.

Real-time example

A 100 MHz package clock pin feeds both chiplets' PLLs; SSC is applied at the source, so both follow it.

If you break it

Separate clocks drift by ppm; the FIFO eventually over- or under-runs.

Check it in the spec
  • SpecREFCLK rules §5.1.1–5.1.2, p.184–185
Clocking§5.5 p.202

Differential clock at 16 GT/s and below, always

Quadrature clocking is an option only at 24 and 32 GT/s; at 16 GT/s and below the differential clock is mandatory.

Why

A design that tops out at 16 GT/s only knows differential clocking. If a faster die used quadrature at 16 GT/s, the slower die could not receive it.

Real-time example

A 32 GT/s die with a quarter-rate receiver uses quadrature at 32 GT/s, but switches to differential when the link trains at 16.

If you break it

No link with lower-rate partners below 24 GT/s.

Check it in the spec
  • SpecClock phase rule §5.5, p.202 · Table 5-12, p.203
Idle§4.1.3 p.101

Postamble before the clock stops

After Valid drops, the clock keeps toggling for 8 cycles (16 UI half-rate, 32 UI quarter-rate) before it may be gated.

Why

The receiver's data path is clocked by the forwarded clock itself. Extra edges after the last data push that data through the receiver's samplers and FIFO before the clock disappears.

Real-time example

A burst ends at UI 15; the clock runs to UI 31, then parks.

If you break it

The last bytes of the burst stay stuck in the receive pipeline until the next burst.

Check it in the spec
  • SpecPostamble §4.1.3, p.101 · §5.11, p.251
  • Derivedpipeline reason
Idle§5.11 p.251–253

Continuous clock at 48 and 64 GT/s

At 48/64 GT/s the forwarded clock must run continuously; clock gating is not used.

Why

At these speeds the clock receiver and I/Q-corrected phases need time to settle; restarting a gated clock would disturb the trained timing. The cost is higher idle power, which the spec accepts (20–25% idle-power target instead of 15%).

Real-time example

A 64 GT/s link sits idle between bursts: Valid low, data preconditioned, clock still at 16 GHz.

If you break it

Timing errors on the first data after idle, causing Valid framing errors and retrains.

Check it in the spec
  • SpecContinuous mode §5.11, p.253 · Table 5-3 note b, p.189
  • Derivedsettling reason
Tx/Rx§5.4.3 p.197

Advanced Package Rx: unterminated up to 32 GT/s

Advanced Package receivers are unterminated up to 32 GT/s and ground-terminated for 48/64 GT/s.

Why

A 2 mm interposer trace has little loss or reflection at lower rates, so skipping termination saves the static current it would draw. At 48/64 GT/s reflections matter and termination pays for itself.

Real-time example

A die-to-die link on a silicon interposer at 16 GT/s runs unterminated; a 64 GT/s die-to-die link on the same interposer terminates to 50 Ω.

If you break it

Terminating at low rate wastes power; leaving 64 GT/s unterminated closes the eye.

Check it in the spec
  • SpecRx termination §5.4.3, p.197
  • Derivedpower/SI reason
Tx/Rx§5.3.4 p.194

Among equal eyes, pick the largest C0

At 48/64 GT/s the Tx preset with the largest eye opening wins; if presets tie, choose the one with the largest C0.

Why

A larger main-cursor coefficient means less de-emphasis, so more signal swing at low frequencies and more margin against noise for the same eye.

Real-time example

P2 (C0 0.90) and P4 (C0 0.80) give equal eyes: choose P2.

If you break it

Not wrong electrically, but less margin than necessary.

Check it in the spec
  • SpecPreset selection §5.3.4, Table 5-7, p.194
  • Derivedmargin reason
BER§5.10 p.251

Raw BER decides whether CRC and retry are needed

At 1E-15 or worse, CRC and retry are required; at 1E-27, parity or CRC with error reporting suffices.

Why

The target is far below 1 failure per 10⁹ device-hours. At 1E-15 and tens of Gb/s, a single lane sees an error every few hours and a wide link every few minutes, so errors must be caught and replayed. At 1E-27 they essentially never happen.

Real-time example

A x64 link at 32 GT/s carries about 2×10¹² bits per second: at 1E-15 that is an error roughly every 8 minutes, each needing a replay.

If you break it

Running Raw Format at 1E-15 without protocol-level protection lets errors through.

Check it in the spec
  • SpecBER and FIT §5.10, Table 5-32, p.251
  • Derivederror interval arithmetic
Sideband§5.13 p.254

Sideband on an always-on supply and clock

The sideband should run from always-on power (VCCAON) and the auxiliary clock.

Why

The sideband trains the mainband, wakes it from low power, and carries management traffic. It has to work while the mainband supply and REFCLK may be off.

Real-time example

While the mainband sits in L1 with its clock stopped, the sideband stays up to carry the exit handshake.

If you break it

A sideband on a switched rail cannot wake the link.

Check it in the spec
  • SpecVCCAON, AUXCLK §5.13, p.254 · §5.6 note, p.205
Degrade§5.7.3.4.1 p.246–247

Standard Package degrades to 1 or 2 modules, never 3

If module pairs fail, the link runs on 1 or 2 modules; one failure on a 4-module link also disables the other pair in the same half.

Why

Power-of-two module counts keep data striping across modules simple and symmetric. Disabling the neighbour in the same half keeps the two surviving modules in a supported pairing.

Real-time example

On a x4 unstacked link, pair M0–M2 fails; M1–M3 next to it is disabled too, and the link runs on the other half as 2 modules.

If you break it

A 3-module link is not a legal configuration.

Check it in the spec
  • SpecDegrade rules, Table 5-29 §5.7.3.4.1, p.246–247
  • Derivedstriping reason
UCIe-3D§6.2 p.258

No Adapter, no CRC, no replay

UCIe-3D has no D2D Adapter and needs no CRC or replay; the SoC logic connects straight to a minimal PHY.

Why

At ≤ 4 GT/s over a near-zero-length vertical bond the BER is below 1E-27, so retry would add area, power and latency for no benefit. At 1 µm pitch the whole lane budget is under 1 µm², with no room for an Adapter.

Real-time example

A cache die bonded on a compute die: SoC logic launches data on a clock edge; the other die samples it half a UI later, 0.125 ns in all.

If you break it

Adding retry to UCIe-3D makes the PHY larger than its bump area.

Check it in the spec
  • SpecObjectives §6.2, p.258–259
  • SpecKPIs Table 6-1, p.259–260
UCIe-3D§6.4.4 p.265

Repair whole bundles, not lanes

UCIe-3D reserves spare modules (bundles) and reroutes around a failed one; dense arrays reserve two full modules per single failure.

Why

Defect size varies with process, pitch and SoC size. At micron pitch one defect can wipe out many adjacent bumps, so per-lane spares, sized for one defect size, would not cover them.

Real-time example

A particle under the stack kills a Tx bundle; SoC logic remaps traffic to a reserved module.

If you break it

Lane-level spares sized for small defects leave yield loss from large ones.

Check it in the spec
  • SpecRepair strategy §6.4.4, p.265–267

Interview-style questions.

Which signals make up one Advanced Package x64 module in one direction? Basic

64 data lanes plus 4 redundant, Valid plus 1 redundant, two clock phases, Track, a redundant lane for clock/Track, and sideband data and clock with a redundant pair.

SpecTable 5-18, p.209–210 · §5.9.1, p.249

What is the forwarded clock frequency at 64 GT/s, and can it be gated? Basic

16 GHz, quarter-rate, quadrature phases. It cannot be gated: at 48/64 GT/s the clock runs continuously.

SpecTable 5-12, p.203 · §5.11, p.253

How long is the clock postamble with half-rate clocking, and why count it in UI? Intermediate

8 clock cycles = 16 UI at half rate (32 UI at quarter rate). Valid framing and gated periods are counted in 8-UI blocks, so UI is the natural unit.

Spec§4.1.3, p.101 · §5.11, p.251

When must a Standard Package receiver be terminated? Intermediate

Whenever the rate and channel length fall outside Table 5-10 (0.4 V swing) or Table 5-11 (0.85 V): for example 16 GT/s needs termination at 0.4 V swing at any length, but at 0.85 V can stay unterminated up to 5 mm.

Spec§5.4.3, Tables 5-10, 5-11, p.197–199

Why does 64 GT/s need CRC and retry on both package types? Intermediate

Its raw BER requirement is 1E-12; anything at 1E-15 or worse needs CRC and retry to reach FIT ≪ 1.

Spec§5.10, Table 5-32, p.251

Name three things UCIe-3D removes compared with UCIe-A. Advanced

The D2D Adapter, CRC/replay, and the required sideband (initialization). The PHY becomes a near-inverter connected to SoC logic, and lane repair becomes bundle repair.
