Where the latency goes. RDI adds no protocol handshakes on the data path: data moves on the cycle all three transfer signals are high, and received bytes move the cycle they are valid. What a design can add is pipeline: the PHY's deskew and multi-module merge, plus the Adapter's CRC. The lp_irdy early hint exists so PHY clock ungating does not add to that.

11 · Test yourself

Questions in the style of a DV interview, from basic to advanced. Answer in your head first, then open the card.

What does pl_inband_pres mean on RDI, and how is that different on FDI? Basic

On RDI it means the PHY has finished link training and is ready for RDI to go Active (Stage 2 complete). On FDI it means the Adapter has finished parameter exchange and the protocol and flit format are known. Same name, one stage apart. On RDI it stays 1 until the LTSM goes to TrainError or Reset.

SpecTable 10-1, p.523 · §3.2.1, p.62

Why must lp_state_req show NOP for a cycle before Active when leaving Reset? Basic

The PHY acts on the edge, not the level. The edge proves the request is fresh and not left over from before a reset, and it lets upper layers hold the link down by delaying the edge (for example while software clears error state).

Spec§10.3.3.1, p.565 · §10.3.3.8, p.569–570

On RDI, which Active exits need pl_stallreq/lp_stallack? How does FDI differ? Basic

RDI: all of them except LinkError, i.e. Retrain, L1/L2, LinkReset and Disabled. FDI in Flit mode uses it only for PM entry and otherwise just drops pl_trdy at a flit boundary. FDI in Raw Format behaves like RDI.

Spec§10.3.2, p.563 · §10.3.3.7, p.568

The PHY detects a Valid framing error. Walk through what happens on RDI. Intermediate

PHY pulses pl_error pipeline-matched with the bad flit or chunk; the Adapter discards it. PHY runs the stall handshake, sends {LinkMgmt.RDI.Req.Retrain}, gets Rsp.Retrain, shows Retrain, exchanges {PHYRETRAIN.retrain start req/resp} and retrains. At LINKINIT the Adapter gives NOP→Active (Retrain was entered from Active), the PHYs swap Req/Rsp.Active, status returns to Active, and Retry replays anything lost.

Spec§4.5.3.7.2, p.170 · Table 10-1 (pl_error), p.524 · §10.3.3.4, p.567

Your Adapter requests L1 and status goes to Active.PMNAK. What now? Intermediate

The Adapter must request Active; status goes back to Active. It may re-request L1 if PM conditions still hold, but the PHY may not send a new PM Request for at least 2 µs after the PMNAK, unless the remote side sends its own PM Request first. Active.PMNAK can never go directly to L1.

Spec§10.1.7, p.535–536 · §10.3, p.559

Why is the LinkError minimum residency 16 ms? Intermediate

It is twice the 8 ms sideband timeout. If the sideband failed and the remote die never got the LinkError message, its own handshakes time out at 8 ms and it goes to LinkError itself. Staying down 16 ms guarantees both dies are down at the same time before either retrains.

Spec§10.3.3.7, p.569 · §10.3.3.8, p.569

Why is clock gating not allowed in LinkError? Intermediate

The spec excludes it on purpose and expects error handlers to move the link out of LinkError if saving power matters. The Adapter needs to see the PHY's status and to drive the exit request, so it must stay clocked.

Spec§10.1.3, p.530

Compare L1 exit and L2 exit on RDI and in the LTSM. Advanced

L1: LTSM goes to MBTRAIN.SPEEDIDLE, RDI goes through Retrain to Active, trained settings and Adapter parameters are kept. L2: LTSM goes to RESET, RDI goes through Reset (needing NOP→Active), training runs in full, and the Adapter repeats parameter exchange. In both cases, Active Request timeouts start only at LINKINIT.

Spec§4.5.3.9, p.172 · §10.1.7, p.536 · §10.3.3.8, p.570

Two stacks share one Adapter. Stack 0 is in LinkReset, stack 1 goes to Disabled. What does RDI do? Advanced

Both Adapter LSMs are now link-down, so RDI moves to the highest-priority one: Disabled (LinkError › Disabled › LinkReset). The PHY sends {LinkMgmt.RDI.Req.Disable}; with no response in 8 ms, RDI goes to LinkError. While only stack 0 was in LinkReset, RDI stayed Active.

Spec§10.3.3.8, p.569 · §3.5, p.90

A 512B RDI receives a single 256B flit and then idle. What does the PHY do? Advanced

It holds the 256B until the link shows a gap of at least 16 UI (differential clock) or 32 UI (quadrature clock) with no valid data, then sends it on RDI bytes 0–255 with bytes 256–511 zero. The Adapter silently drops the zero half, because a flit header of 0000h with CRC 0000h is discarded and not counted for Runtime Link Testing.

Spec§10.3.1 Implementation Note, p.562

How does a 68B flit look different on FDI and on RDI? Advanced

On FDI only the 64B payload crosses; the Adapter adds the 2-byte Flit Header and 2-byte CRC below. On RDI the full 68B flit crosses, packed back to back (a 128B RDI carries Flit 0 bytes 0–67 then Flit 1 bytes 0–59), and the stream is closed with PDS before a pause.

SpecFigures 10-28 and 10-29, p.560–561 · §10.3.1, p.560

Can the PHY send {LinkMgmt.RDI.Rsp.Active} as soon as it receives Req.Active? Advanced

No. It must also have sampled lp_state_req = Active on its own RDI. Otherwise the remote Adapter would start Stage 3 parameter exchange with an Adapter that is not ready. Status goes to Active only after the PHY has both sent and received Rsp.Active.

Spec§10.1.6 steps 3–5, p.534
