UCIe Electrical Layer and UCIe-3D Field Guide

Chapter 5 is 74 pages of tables keyed by package type and data rate; most questions are really "what applies at my rate on my package?". This guide sorts the electrical rules by that question, with a lookup tool, then covers UCIe-3D, which drops the Adapter and most of the PHY for stacked dies. 1 · What the electrical layer pins down

Interoperability

Any two compliant dies must link

Every device supports 4 GT/s and every rate between 4 GT/s and its maximum, so two dies with different maximums always share rates. Bump maps fix signal order so modules from different vendors line up.

Spec§5.1.1, p.184

Simplicity

Short, clean, single-ended

Single-ended, unidirectional, DC-coupled point-to-point lanes with a forwarded clock and length-matched routing within a module. The forwarded clock replaces clock recovery, and the short channel allows a small, low-power PHY.

Spec§5.0, p.184

Two families

Advanced (UCIe-A) vs Standard (UCIe-S)

Advanced Package (silicon bridge or interposer, e.g. EMIB, CoWoS): fine bump pitch, 2 mm reach, 64 or 32 lanes per module. Standard Package (organic substrate): 100–130 µm pitch, 10–25 mm reach, 16 or 8 lanes.

Spec§5.2.1, p.186 · Tables 1-1, 1-2, p.40

UCIe-3D

Stacked dies, almost no PHY

Hybrid-bonded stacks at ≤ 10 µm pitch run at up to 4 GT/s (the SoC clock) with no Adapter, no CRC or replay and a near-inverter PHY. The design goal is energy and area per bit, not reach.

Spec§6.2, p.258–259

x32 and x8 variants

An x32 Advanced module has 32 data lanes plus 2 redundant. An x8 Standard module has 8 data lanes. Up to 4 modules can be grouped for bandwidth.

Spec§5.3, p.190 · §5.2.1, p.186

Mixing x64 and x32

Optional for an x64 module. When used, the x32 always connects to the lower half of the x64; one wide module may not talk to two narrow ones at once.

Spec§5.7.2.4, p.228

Protect the receiver

Keep the Tx output high (VOH) within 100 mV above the receiving chiplet's front-end supply; an over-stress protection circuit may be used if it is higher.

4 · Clocking

Common reference clock

  • Both dies of a link use REFCLK from one source (package pin or forwarded by another die). 100 MHz recommended; 25–200 MHz allowed; ±300 ppm.
  • SSC allowed: 30–33 kHz modulation, 0 to −0.5% down-spread, ≤ 1250 ppm/µs slew.
  • Tx-to-Rx transport delay difference must be under 5 ns.
  • For a retimer, the off-package link need not share REFCLK; the local UCIe link must.
  • Link Speed settings may be run slower: e.g. the 32 GT/s setting covers 24–32 GT/s by adjusting REFCLK or the PLL divider (only while the link is down).

Differential at 16 GT/s and below, always

At 24 and 32 GT/s the receiver chooses differential or quadrature clocking (advertised in PHY Capability). At 16 GT/s and below, differential must always be used so the link works with designs whose maximum is lower.

Spec§5.5, p.202 · Table 9-47, p.492

Track

On request over sideband, the partner sends a clock-like pattern on its Track lane, aligned to Phase-1: 1010… with half-rate clocking, 1100… with quarter-rate. The receiver uses it to correct slow voltage, temperature and aging drift between data and clock.

Spec§5.5.1, p.202 · §5.3, p.190

6 · Transmitter and receiver

Transmitter

  • Data and clock swing at least 0.4 V. Driver impedance 25 Ω ± 3 (Advanced), 30 Ω ± 3 (Standard).
  • Phase adjustment (deskew) lives on the transmitter; training sets it for best margin.
  • Tri-state outputs in low-power states.
  • 24/32 GT/s EQ (must support; recommended at 16): de-emphasis only, settings 0 dB and −2.2 dB (C+1 = −0.112).
  • 48/64 GT/s EQ (required): 3-tap FIR presets P0–P5. Pick the largest eye; on a tie, the largest C0.

Receiver

  • Clock and data paths delay-matched to reject supply-noise jitter; 2-way or 4-way interleaved.
  • Rx EQ may be used at 24/32 GT/s (about a 1st-order CTLE). At 48/64 GT/s Rx EQ is required with Tx EQ: reference CTLE with at least 7 settings from −6 to 0 dB in 1 dB steps; optional 1-tap DFE with h1/h0 < 0.35.
  • Rx input impedance 50 Ω ± 5 at 48/64 GT/s.

Spec§5.4, p.195 · §5.4.4, p.200–201 · Table 5-9, p.197

Rx termination

  • Advanced: unterminated up to 32 GT/s; terminated to ground for 48/64 GT/s negotiated maximum.
  • Standard, 0.4 V swing: unterminated recommended only up to 12 GT/s at 3 mm, 8 GT/s at 5 mm, 4 GT/s at 10 mm.
  • Standard, 0.85 V swing: 16 GT/s at 5 mm, 12 GT/s at 10 mm, 8 GT/s and below at any supported length.
  • Otherwise terminate to ground.

Spec§5.4.3, Tables 5-10, 5-11, p.197–199

7 · The channel

Eye masks

Checked by channel simulation with ideal (noiseless, jitterless) behavioral Tx/Rx and the 19 largest aggressors. Rectangular mask ≤ 32 GT/s: 40 mV high and 0.75 UI wide up to 16 GT/s, 0.65 UI at 24/32 (with EQ). Diamond mask at 48/64 GT/s: 65 mV and 0.65 UI, searching only presets P0–P5 and the reference CTLE.

Spec§5.7, Table 5-15, p.205–207

Loss and crosstalk (VTF)

Voltage Transfer Function metrics include Tx/Rx termination. Advanced: L(fN) > −3 dB at 4–16 GT/s, > −5 dB at 24/32; crosstalk below a line in loss and below −23/−24 dB. Standard (terminated): L(fN) > −7.5 dB at 4/8 and 24/32, > −6.5 dB at 12/16, with L(0) > −4.5 dB.

Spec§5.7.1, Table 5-17, p.208–209 · Tables 5-24, 5-25, p.235

Lane matching

Allowed channel mismatch around the lane centre: Advanced ±0.03 UI (4–32 GT/s), ±0.06 UI (48/64); Standard ±0.07, ±0.10, ±0.20 UI for 4–16, 24/32, 48/64 GT/s. It is the gap between Tx deskew range and Rx skew tolerance.

SpecTable 5-16, p.208

Bump pitch limits speed (Advanced)

Recommended: 16-column map at 25–30 µm up to 12 GT/s and 31–37 µm up to 16; 10-column at 38–44 µm up to 24 and 45–50 µm up to 32; 8-column at 51–55 µm up to 32 GT/s. Check with the packaging vendor.

SpecTable 5-19, p.215

8 · Idle, clock gating and BER

Going idle

  • After Valid goes low, keep the clock running for a postamble of 8 cycles: 16 UI half-rate, 32 UI quarter-rate. Then gate it, unless free-running clock was negotiated.
  • Valid held low; data lanes hold the last bit (some may keep toggling like scrambled data without advancing the scrambler).
  • Strobe mode: the parked clock level alternates between high and low on consecutive gating events. Before resuming, drive clock differential low for 1–8 UI. Gated period is a multiple of 8 UI.
  • 48/64 GT/s: the clock always runs (continuous mode).
  • Electrical idle (some training states): Tx/Rx on, data, Valid and Track low, clock parked.

Spec§4.1.3, p.101 · §5.11, p.251–253 · §5.12, p.253

Raw BER sets the error handling

At BER 1E-15 or worse, CRC and retry are required to reach FIT ≪ 1. At 1E-27, parity or CRC with error reporting is enough. 64 GT/s is 1E-12 on both package types.

Spec§5.10, Table 5-32, p.251

Valid protection at 48/64 GT/s

Valid framing uses Hamming-distance-4 encodings. At 48 and 64 GT/s receivers must correct single-bit and detect double-bit Valid errors, which keeps Valid errors from causing frequent retrains.

Spec§5.11, p.253

Spec inconsistency to note at 48 GT/s

§4.1.2.1 (retimer Valid framing) lets receivers at ≤ 48 GT/s retrain on any Valid error instead of correcting. §5.11 requires single-error correction at 48 GT/s. Implementing correction at 48 GT/s satisfies both.

Derived §4.1.2.1, p.101 vs §5.11, p.253

9 · Sideband, AUXCLK and Open Drain

Sideband

800 MT/s SDR on an always-on rail

Data is launched edge-aligned with the strobe's rising edge and sampled on its falling edge. Runs from the always-on supply VCCAON (0.65 V in Table 5-33) and AUXCLK. Setup and hold 200 ps. Same reach as the mainband; Standard Package has an extended-reach option up to 100 mm (60 Ω driver). Redundancy and repair only on Advanced Package.

Spec§5.13, Table 5-33, p.254

AUXCLK

Relaxed clock for the sideband

Any source; 100 MHz recommended, 25–800 MHz allowed, SSC permitted. REFCLK can serve if it is always on. With a frequency-adjusted REFCLK the sideband runs 400–800 MT/s and its 8 ms timeouts scale to 16 ms at 400 MT/s.

Spec§5.13.2, Table 5-34, p.255 · note, p.189

Open Drain

A shared low-latency wire

Optional pin on a package-wide net with one pull-up (5–10 kΩ); any chiplet pulls low (120–240 Ω). Fall to VOL < 100 ns, rise to VOH < 2 µs. Used for UCIe low-latency events such as Fast Throttle, and vendor events.

Spec§5.14, Table 5-35, p.255–256

Sideband-only port

For test and manageability

A UCIe-S port may be sideband only. It uses the same RDI signals as a sideband with a mainband.

Spec§5.7.4, p.248

10 · Repair (Advanced) and degrade (Standard)

Advanced: spare bumps

Each module has 4 redundant data bumps, 1 for clock and Track, 1 for Valid. On x64 the data spares are split two per half (TRD_P0/P1 for lanes 0–31, TRD_P2/P3 for 32–63). Sideband has a redundant data and clock pair. The logical remap is in the Logical PHY guide.

Spec§5.9.1, p.249–250 · Table 5-18, p.209

Standard: drop whole module pairs

  • A degraded link is 1 or 2 modules, never 3.
  • 4-module link: 1 or 2 failed pairs → 2 modules; 3 failed → 1 module.
  • 2-module link: 1 failed pair → 1 module.
  • On a 4-module link with one failure, the other pair in the same half (along the die edge) is also disabled.

Spec§5.7.3.4.1, Table 5-29, p.246–247

11 · UCIe-3D

Design rules

Fit inside the bump area

Circuit and logic must fit under the bumps (at 1 µm pitch, under 1 µm² per lane). No D2D Adapter, no CRC or replay. The PHY is a simple driver/inverter connected directly to SoC logic. Test and debug hooks sit in one common block per chiplet. Lane repair becomes bundle repair run by SoC logic.

Spec§6.2, p.258–259

Timing

Launch on rise, sample on fall

Data and clock buffers are delay-matched, so the data/clock differential is near zero and sampling half a UI later (rise-to-fall) gives the best margin. Unlike the 2D matched architecture, the 3D transceiver avoids its power cost while keeping supply-noise rejection.

Spec§6.3, p.260 · Figure 6-4, p.261

Budget

Everything must fit in 1 UI

Channel eye closure (0.1 UI), pulse-width deviation (0.08), Tx and Rx lane skew (0.12 each), RSS of Tx/Rx random jitter (0.05 each), aperture (0.03) and the Vcc-dependent spread of Tx/Rx data-clock delay (50 ps each, scaled by 1 + α·noise) must sum to less than 1 UI (250 ps at 4 GT/s). With the table's values the sum is about 0.98 UI, so there is almost no slack.

Spec§6.4.1, Table 6-2, Equation 6-6, p.261–263 · Derived sum

Module

x80 bundles, 9 µm recommended

A module is one Tx bundle (x80 + clock) and one Rx bundle. About 0.02 mm² at 9 µm pitch, scaling with pitch squared. x70 also defined. No sideband required; lanes may be assigned to Valid, mask, parity or ECC as needed. Tx swing 0.40–0.75 V. ESD 5 V CDM (≤ 10 µm pitch).

Spec§6.4.2–6.4.3, Tables 6-3, 6-4, p.263–265

Bundle repair calculator

The spec's model: reserve 2k full modules, where k is the smallest value with 1 − Σi=0..k Pi(A·D0) < δ, Pi being Poisson. Check also that large defects are rare: 1 − e−A·D1 < δ.

Spec§6.4.4, Equations 6-7 to 6-9, p.265–267 · Illustrative default inputs

12 · Why each rule exists

Interop§5.1.1 p.184

Support every rate from 4 GT/s up to your maximum

A device supporting 16 GT/s must also run 4, 8 and 12 GT/s.

Why

Two dies from different vendors always share 4 GT/s, and training can step down through every rate when a channel cannot hold the top one (speed degrade in LINKSPEED).

Real-time example

A 32 GT/s die meets a 16 GT/s die: they train at 16. Later the channel degrades and the link falls back to 12 GT/s.

If you break it

A die that skips 12 GT/s strands its partner at 8 GT/s when 16 fails.

Check it in the spec
  • SpecData rates §5.1.1, p.184
Clocking§5.1.1 p.184

One REFCLK, under 5 ns of transport delay difference

Transmitter and partner receiver share a reference clock from one source, with Tx-to-Rx transport delay difference under 5 ns.

Why

With the same reference, both ends run at exactly the same frequency, so the receive FIFO only absorbs phase, not frequency drift. Limiting the delay difference keeps that FIFO shallow and its latency low.

Real-time example

A 100 MHz package clock pin feeds both chiplets' PLLs; SSC is applied at the source, so both follow it.

If you break it

Separate clocks drift by ppm; the FIFO eventually over- or under-runs.

Check it in the spec
  • SpecREFCLK rules §5.1.1–5.1.2, p.184–185
Clocking§5.5 p.202

Differential clock at 16 GT/s and below, always

Quadrature clocking is an option only at 24 and 32 GT/s; at 16 GT/s and below the differential clock is mandatory.

Why

A design that tops out at 16 GT/s only knows differential clocking. If a faster die used quadrature at 16 GT/s, the slower die could not receive it.

Real-time example

A 32 GT/s die with a quarter-rate receiver uses quadrature at 32 GT/s, but switches to differential when the link trains at 16.

If you break it

No link with lower-rate partners below 24 GT/s.

Check it in the spec
  • SpecClock phase rule §5.5, p.202 · Table 5-12, p.203
Idle§4.1.3 p.101

Postamble before the clock stops

After Valid drops, the clock keeps toggling for 8 cycles (16 UI half-rate, 32 UI quarter-rate) before it may be gated.

Why

The receiver's data path is clocked by the forwarded clock itself. Extra edges after the last data push that data through the receiver's samplers and FIFO before the clock disappears.

Real-time example

A burst ends at UI 15; the clock runs to UI 31, then parks.

If you break it

The last bytes of the burst stay stuck in the receive pipeline until the next burst.

Check it in the spec
  • SpecPostamble §4.1.3, p.101 · §5.11, p.251
  • Derivedpipeline reason
Idle§5.11 p.251–253

Continuous clock at 48 and 64 GT/s

At 48/64 GT/s the forwarded clock must run continuously; clock gating is not used.

Why

At these speeds the clock receiver and I/Q-corrected phases need time to settle; restarting a gated clock would disturb the trained timing. The cost is higher idle power, which the spec accepts (20–25% idle-power target instead of 15%).

Real-time example

A 64 GT/s link sits idle between bursts: Valid low, data preconditioned, clock still at 16 GHz.

If you break it

Timing errors on the first data after idle, causing Valid framing errors and retrains.

Check it in the spec
  • SpecContinuous mode §5.11, p.253 · Table 5-3 note b, p.189
  • Derivedsettling reason
Tx/Rx§5.4.3 p.197

Advanced Package Rx: unterminated up to 32 GT/s

Advanced Package receivers are unterminated up to 32 GT/s and ground-terminated for 48/64 GT/s.

Why

A 2 mm interposer trace has little loss or reflection at lower rates, so skipping termination saves the static current it would draw. At 48/64 GT/s reflections matter and termination pays for itself.

Real-time example

A die-to-die link on a silicon interposer at 16 GT/s runs unterminated; a 64 GT/s die-to-die link on the same interposer terminates to 50 Ω.

If you break it

Terminating at low rate wastes power; leaving 64 GT/s unterminated closes the eye.

Check it in the spec
  • SpecRx termination §5.4.3, p.197
  • Derivedpower/SI reason
Tx/Rx§5.3.4 p.194

Among equal eyes, pick the largest C0

At 48/64 GT/s the Tx preset with the largest eye opening wins; if presets tie, choose the one with the largest C0.

Why

A larger main-cursor coefficient means less de-emphasis, so more signal swing at low frequencies and more margin against noise for the same eye.

Real-time example

P2 (C0 0.90) and P4 (C0 0.80) give equal eyes: choose P2.

If you break it

Not wrong electrically, but less margin than necessary.

Check it in the spec
  • SpecPreset selection §5.3.4, Table 5-7, p.194
  • Derivedmargin reason
BER§5.10 p.251

Raw BER decides whether CRC and retry are needed

At 1E-15 or worse, CRC and retry are required; at 1E-27, parity or CRC with error reporting suffices.

Why

The target is far below 1 failure per 10⁹ device-hours. At 1E-15 and tens of Gb/s, a single lane sees an error every few hours and a wide link every few minutes, so errors must be caught and replayed. At 1E-27 they essentially never happen.

Real-time example

A x64 link at 32 GT/s carries about 2×10¹² bits per second: at 1E-15 that is an error roughly every 8 minutes, each needing a replay.

If you break it

Running Raw Format at 1E-15 without protocol-level protection lets errors through.

Check it in the spec
  • SpecBER and FIT §5.10, Table 5-32, p.251
  • Derivederror interval arithmetic
Sideband§5.13 p.254

Sideband on an always-on supply and clock

The sideband should run from always-on power (VCCAON) and the auxiliary clock.

Why

The sideband trains the mainband, wakes it from low power, and carries management traffic. It has to work while the mainband supply and REFCLK may be off.

Real-time example

While the mainband sits in L1 with its clock stopped, the sideband stays up to carry the exit handshake.

If you break it

A sideband on a switched rail cannot wake the link.

Check it in the spec
  • SpecVCCAON, AUXCLK §5.13, p.254 · §5.6 note, p.205
Degrade§5.7.3.4.1 p.246–247

Standard Package degrades to 1 or 2 modules, never 3

If module pairs fail, the link runs on 1 or 2 modules; one failure on a 4-module link also disables the other pair in the same half.

Why

Power-of-two module counts keep data striping across modules simple and symmetric. Disabling the neighbour in the same half keeps the two surviving modules in a supported pairing.

Real-time example

On a x4 unstacked link, pair M0–M2 fails; M1–M3 next to it is disabled too, and the link runs on the other half as 2 modules.

If you break it

A 3-module link is not a legal configuration.

Check it in the spec
  • SpecDegrade rules, Table 5-29 §5.7.3.4.1, p.246–247
  • Derivedstriping reason
UCIe-3D§6.2 p.258

No Adapter, no CRC, no replay

UCIe-3D has no D2D Adapter and needs no CRC or replay; the SoC logic connects straight to a minimal PHY.

Why

At ≤ 4 GT/s over a near-zero-length vertical bond the BER is below 1E-27, so retry would add area, power and latency for no benefit. At 1 µm pitch the whole lane budget is under 1 µm², with no room for an Adapter.

Real-time example

A cache die bonded on a compute die: SoC logic launches data on a clock edge; the other die samples it half a UI later, 0.125 ns in all.

If you break it

Adding retry to UCIe-3D makes the PHY larger than its bump area.

Check it in the spec
  • SpecObjectives §6.2, p.258–259
  • SpecKPIs Table 6-1, p.259–260
UCIe-3D§6.4.4 p.265

Repair whole bundles, not lanes

UCIe-3D reserves spare modules (bundles) and reroutes around a failed one; dense arrays reserve two full modules per single failure.

Why

Defect size varies with process, pitch and SoC size. At micron pitch one defect can wipe out many adjacent bumps, so per-lane spares, sized for one defect size, would not cover them.

Real-time example

A particle under the stack kills a Tx bundle; SoC logic remaps traffic to a reserved module.

If you break it

Lane-level spares sized for small defects leave yield loss from large ones.

Check it in the spec
  • SpecRepair strategy §6.4.4, p.265–267

14 · Test yourself

Interview-style questions.

Which signals make up one Advanced Package x64 module in one direction? Basic

64 data lanes plus 4 redundant, Valid plus 1 redundant, two clock phases, Track, a redundant lane for clock/Track, and sideband data and clock with a redundant pair.

SpecTable 5-18, p.209–210 · §5.9.1, p.249

What is the forwarded clock frequency at 64 GT/s, and can it be gated? Basic

16 GHz, quarter-rate, quadrature phases. It cannot be gated: at 48/64 GT/s the clock runs continuously.

SpecTable 5-12, p.203 · §5.11, p.253

How long is the clock postamble with half-rate clocking, and why count it in UI? Intermediate

8 clock cycles = 16 UI at half rate (32 UI at quarter rate). Valid framing and gated periods are counted in 8-UI blocks, so UI is the natural unit.

Spec§4.1.3, p.101 · §5.11, p.251

When must a Standard Package receiver be terminated? Intermediate

Whenever the rate and channel length fall outside Table 5-10 (0.4 V swing) or Table 5-11 (0.85 V): for example 16 GT/s needs termination at 0.4 V swing at any length, but at 0.85 V can stay unterminated up to 5 mm.

Spec§5.4.3, Tables 5-10, 5-11, p.197–199

Why does 64 GT/s need CRC and retry on both package types? Intermediate

Its raw BER requirement is 1E-12; anything at 1E-15 or worse needs CRC and retry to reach FIT ≪ 1.

Spec§5.10, Table 5-32, p.251

Name three things UCIe-3D removes compared with UCIe-A. Advanced

The D2D Adapter, CRC/replay, and the required sideband (initialization). The PHY becomes a near-inverter connected to SoC logic, and lane repair becomes bundle repair.

Spec§6.2, p.258–259 · §6.4.3, p.265
